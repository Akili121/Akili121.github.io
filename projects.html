2026 · Flood impact assessment
Assessing Flood Impacts on Highway Networks with Graph Neural Networks

Graph neural networks · highway networks · flood disruption · multidimensional infrastructure data

This project develops a graph neural network framework that combines roadway structure, traffic operations, flood exposure, and community characteristics to assess how flooding disrupts highway networks. The model predicts post-flood traffic flow and speed and translates those changes into a Disaster Impact Index and a road-segment Criticality Score.

Applied to Harris County during Hurricane Harvey, the framework distinguishes direct impacts on inundated high-demand roads from indirect disruption that propagates through the network. It identifies critical corridors while accounting for the social vulnerability of the communities they serve, providing a more complete basis for preparedness and response planning.

Equity-Informed Post-Disaster Highway Restoration

social equity · post-disaster recovery · graph neural networks · restoration prioritization

This project introduces the Equity-Based Resilience Index, a road-segment measure that links functional recovery needs with the social vulnerability of affected communities. A graph neural network learns this index from disaster, structural, operational, and socioeconomic information, turning equity objectives into actionable restoration priorities.

The Hurricane Harvey case study shows that a traffic-only strategy can overlook lower-demand roads that are essential to vulnerable communities. Jointly considering network performance and social vulnerability produces a more balanced recovery sequence, accelerating functional restoration while better aligning priorities with equity objectives.

2025 · Human and organizational resilience
Modeling Contractors’ Mental Resilience under Disruptive Events

agent-based modeling · mental resilience · urban rail transit · land subsidence

This project uses agent-based modeling to examine how contractors’ mental resilience evolves during disruptive events in urban rail transit construction. The model represents interactions among hazard experience, risk perception, flexibility, cooperation, information seeking, and fear, and is evaluated through a land-subsidence case from Jinan Metro Line 2.

The findings identify fear as a central mechanism and reveal a behavioral pathway from prior experience and risk perception to adaptive action, collaboration, information exchange, and reduced fear. The results support targeted management strategies for strengthening workforce resilience during construction emergencies.
